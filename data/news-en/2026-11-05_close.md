## 1. Market narrative

**Close facts.** The Dow fell 1,833.06 points to 51,523.06 (-3.4%), the S&P 500 lost 334.89 to 7,353.73 (-4.4%), and the Nasdaq dropped 1,421.52 to 24,315.00 (-5.5%). The Nasdaq is 11.9% below last month's high, conventionally a correction. Wednesday's premarket-to-close narrative—less election uncertainty and lower oil—gave way to a reassessment of AI capex returns, financing and construction risk. [[SOURCE|AP final index close|2026-11-05 16:15 ET|event date 2026-11-05|https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-ai-midterm-oil-trump-7ee9c212805aa5e70cf34d25aab7a36e]]

**Interpretation, not fact.** This was not a simple higher-rates growth selloff: the 10-year yield fell from 4.90% Wednesday to 4.84%. The better working inference is that investors marked down durability and capital returns in the AI supply chain despite a lower discount rate. That inference fails if orders, utilization and customer funding hold up and valuations rapidly recover. A single down day does not establish that the AI investment cycle is over. ## 2. Major gainers

AppLovin rose 31%, the clearest counterexample to indiscriminate risk reduction. Its November 4 release reported Q3 revenue of $2.185 billion (+55% year over year), net income of $1.439 billion (+72%), and a $3.2 billion increase in authorization, bringing buyback capacity to $3.6 billion. The reaction says verified growth, margins and capital return could still be rewarded; it does not generalize to all software or AI exposures. ## 5. Emerging theme

The theme is shifting from “AI demand exists” to who pays construction costs and who has verifiable free cash flow. AppLovin's reported cash generation and buyback are a positive example; capital-intensive chips, power and cloud-rental chains were asked to prove order durability. Discussion of potential data-center restrictions after the election is a constraint risk, not an implemented policy.
